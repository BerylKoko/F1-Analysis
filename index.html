First, define “normal.”
For Hamilton, that is the problem.

02 / AFTER DOMINANCE

Then the car stopped being the benchmark.

Mercedes still won the 2021 Constructors' title. After the 2022 regulation reset, it finished 3rd, 2nd and 4th across 2022–2024. Hamilton's headline results fell at the same time: no wins in 2022 or 2023, then two in 2024.

That does not prove the car caused the decline. It does mean wins alone cannot isolate the driver.

04 / THE FERRARI TEST

2025 looked like the ending.
2026 makes it look like a transition.

Hamilton's first Ferrari season was his first without a Grand Prix podium: sixth in the championship, 156 points, and heavily beaten by Charles Leclerc in qualifying and comparable races. Ferrari itself fell from second in 2024 to fourth in 2025 and won no Grand Prix.

Through 14 Grands Prix in 2026, Hamilton is third in the championship with 191 total points, one Grand Prix win and five Grand Prix podiums. Ferrari's intra-team fight is even in qualifying, while Hamilton leads the race head-to-head.

WHAT THE DATA SUPPORTS

Decline? Yes — but not one clean decline.

01

The results declined.

The dominant-era win, podium and pole rates did not survive the post-2021 period. That part is measurable and substantial.

02

The baseline was extraordinary.

“Decline” starts from seven seasons in which more than half of Hamilton's starts became wins. Comparing every later season to that peak magnifies the visual fall.

03

The car changed with the results.

Mercedes fell away from championship-winning machinery after 2021; Ferrari then finished fourth in Hamilton's first season. Absolute results cannot separate those effects.

04

Teammate evidence is uneven.

2024–25 provide genuine evidence of relative weakness, especially qualifying. But the 2026 rebound is inconsistent with a simple, steady year-by-year deterioration story.

The McQueen Effect is not “Hamilton never declined.” It is that peak dominance is a terrible neutral baseline. The more defensible story is a decline in some dimensions, amplified by machinery and adaptation — with enough recent recovery to reject the simplest version of “he is just fading.”

METHOD

What I measured

Outcome rates
Wins, podiums and poles divided by Grand Prix starts.

Teammate comparison
Qualifying H2H, race H2H and share of teammate-pair championship points.

Car context
Constructor championship position beside driver outcomes.

Limits
Teammates are not perfect controls; 2026 is incomplete; head-to-head rules differ by source.
